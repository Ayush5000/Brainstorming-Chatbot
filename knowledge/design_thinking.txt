DESIGN THINKING

Design Thinking is a human-centered approach to solving problems.

The common stages are:

1. Empathize
2. Define
3. Ideate
4. Prototype
5. Test

EMPATHIZE

Understand the people experiencing the problem.

Questions:

- Who is affected?
- What are they trying to accomplish?
- What problems do they experience?
- What frustrates them?
- What do they currently do?
- What would make their experience better?

DEFINE

Convert observations into a clear problem.

A good problem statement identifies:

- target user
- user need
- important difficulty
- desired improvement

Example structure:

"How might we help [user] achieve [goal] despite
[problem or constraint]?"

IDEATE

Generate multiple possible solutions before choosing one.

Useful ideation prompts:

- What are ten possible approaches?
- What would a simple solution look like?
- What would an advanced solution look like?
- What would an AI-powered solution look like?
- What would a low-cost solution look like?
- What would a completely different solution look like?

PROTOTYPE

Think about the smallest version of the solution that could
demonstrate the core idea.

Ask:

- What is the minimum useful version?
- What feature is absolutely necessary?
- What can be postponed?
- How could the idea be tested quickly?

TEST

Think about how the idea could be validated.

Ask:

- Who should test it?
- What assumption needs validation?
- What result would indicate success?
- What feedback should be collected?

DESIGN THINKING IN BRAINSTORMING

When the user provides a human-centered problem, use Design
Thinking to generate questions about users, needs, pain points,
solutions and validation.

Do not force all five stages into every response.

Use only the relevant stages.
